faq:
  - q: DisplayPort vs HDMI, what's the difference?
    a: Both carry picture and sound. DisplayPort is mostly found on computers, graphics cards and docking stations, while HDMI is on almost every TV, projector and most monitors. A DisplayPort to HDMI cable simply lets a computer's DisplayPort output drive an HDMI screen.
  - q: Does this cable work both ways?
    a: No. It only converts from DisplayPort (computer end) to HDMI (screen end). If your computer has HDMI and your monitor has DisplayPort, you need a different, active adapter.
  - q: Does it support 4K?
    a: Yes, up to 3840×2160. Its listed bandwidth is HDMI 1.4 level, which normally means 4K at 30Hz. That's fine for documents and video, but for smooth 4K at 60Hz you need an active DisplayPort to HDMI 2.0 adapter.
  - q: Does it carry sound?
    a: Yes. It carries audio as well as video, so a TV's or monitor's speakers will play sound from your computer. You may need to choose the TV or monitor as the sound output on the computer.
  - q: Why is my DisplayPort to HDMI cable not working?
    a: Check the cable goes from the computer's DisplayPort to the screen's HDMI, not the other way round. It's a passive cable, so the computer's DisplayPort must support dual-mode (DP++); most do, but some older or business graphics cards don't. Also select the right HDMI input on the screen.
  - q: Will it work with my laptop?
    a: Only if your laptop has a full-size DisplayPort socket. Many laptops have USB-C, Thunderbolt or Mini DisplayPort instead, and need a USB-C to HDMI or Mini DisplayPort to HDMI cable.

A DisplayPort to HDMI cable is one of those small bits of kit that saves the day. Plenty of desktop PCs, graphics cards and docking stations have spare DisplayPort outputs, while most monitors, TVs and projectors use HDMI. This Multibao cable connects the two directly, with no adapter box in between, and it's cheap enough to keep a spare in the drawer.

It supports resolutions up to 4K (3840×2160), including 1440p and 1080p, and carries audio as well as video, so a TV's or monitor's speakers will play sound. It's HDCP compliant, so streaming services work, and it comes in 1, 2 and 3 metre lengths. The 1 metre version suits a PC under the desk running up to a monitor.

Two things to know before you buy. It only works one way, from DisplayPort on the computer to HDMI on the screen. And it's a simple passive cable with HDMI 1.4-level bandwidth, so 4K normally runs at 30Hz: great for office work and video, but gamers wanting 4K at 60Hz should choose an active adapter instead. For everyday office screens, it's a simple, inexpensive fix.
